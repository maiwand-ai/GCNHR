Who we are

A platform for dialogue, research and cooperation

GCNHR was founded in Geneva in 2026 as a non-profit association under Articles 60 and following of the Swiss Civil Code. Our activities serve the public interest and humanitarian purposes only, never commercial goals.

We bring together Afghans, civil society organisations, human rights defenders, legal professionals, researchers, academics and international organisations to share knowledge, document realities and pursue peaceful solutions.

Our mission

Eight strategic objectives

Our statutes set out eight principal objectives that guide every programme, partnership and publication.

01

Afghan women's human rights

Supporting the fundamental rights, freedoms, dignity, equality and meaningful participation of Afghan women and girls.

02

Negotiation

Facilitating dialogue, consultation and peaceful engagement among Afghans and with civil society, legal experts, researchers and international institutions.

03

Documentation and research

Researching, documenting and analysing the human rights situation of Afghans in Afghanistan and abroad, and publishing reports and studies.

04

Peace, social justice and non-discrimination

Supporting peaceful, civic approaches to preventing and resolving conflict, and preventing violence, discrimination, extremism and injustice.

05

Children's rights

Promoting the rights of Afghan children, and girls in particular, to education, freedom, dignity and protection.

06

Amplifying Afghan voices

Bringing the legitimate concerns, experiences and aspirations of Afghans into international discussions, conferences and policy forums.

07

Cooperation in international Geneva

Building constructive cooperation with the United Nations, international organisations, diplomatic missions, academia and human rights bodies.

08

Networks among Afghans

Strengthening networks among Afghans, civil society, researchers and human rights defenders in Switzerland and beyond.

What we do

From objectives to action

Our activities combine dialogue, evidence and advocacy, carried out with partners in Switzerland and around the world.

Negotiation platforms

Meetings, consultations, roundtables and conferences on Afghanistan and human rights.

Research and documentation

Reports, studies and policy papers based on carefully gathered, publicly available information.

Advocacy and awareness

Public discussions, campaigns and education on human rights, women's rights and children's rights.

International engagement

Taking part in United Nations and international forums in Geneva and elsewhere.

Partnership and cooperation

Working with Swiss and international organisations, universities, research institutes and legal professionals.

Publications

Reports, research papers, articles, policy analyses, statements and newsletters.

Capacity building and training

Seminars, workshops and training in human rights, dialogue, negotiation, research and civic participation.

Networking and community

Platforms connecting Afghans, human rights defenders, civil society and researchers.

Promoting peaceful negotiation

Initiatives for mutual understanding, reconciliation, social cohesion and non-violent solutions.

Our principles

How we work

Independence

We accept no support that would compromise our independence, impartiality or credibility.

Integrity and transparency

We manage our finances responsibly and transparently, in line with Swiss law.

Equality and non-discrimination

Human rights, equality and peaceful dialogue guide everything we do.

Public benefit

All our resources serve our statutory purposes. No profits are ever distributed.

Governance

Accountable and democratic

Our bodies work together to ensure transparency, accountability and democratic decision-making.

  1. 1

    General Assembly

    Our highest authority. It meets at least once a year to approve reports and accounts, elect the Management Committee and set our strategic direction.

  2. 2

    Management Committee

    At least three members, elected for renewable two-year terms, who carry out the Assembly's decisions and run our programmes.

  3. 3

    Independent audit

    An independent auditor or financial reviewer examines the annual accounts and reports to the General Assembly.
